# machado2022alumni
**Full citation:** Machado, Cecilia, German Reyes, and Evan Riehl (2022). Alumni Job Networks at Elite Universities and the Efficacy of Affirmative Action. IZA Discussion Paper No. 15026. (Published as Journal of Labor Economics, 2025, 43(2), 391-431.)
**DOI / URL:** https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/15026 (SSRN abstract 4114556)
**Access level:** abstract only (read IZA and SSRN abstract pages; full PDF not retrieved)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper makes the alumni/employer network channel — the mechanism behind the ER side of our gap — concrete and measurable with matched employer-employee data. It shows that a major payoff to attending an elite university is access to high-paying firms staffed by its own alumni, which is exactly the labor-market behavioral signal our Employer Reputation captures and which need not track faculty-hiring prestige. For the project's hypothesis that credential/network fields (law, finance) are high-gap, this is direct evidence that institutional reputation operates through who-hires-whom networks rather than through skill value-added. It also flags a within-institution distributional caveat we should respect: network value is congestible (diversity raised access for disadvantaged students but lowered top-ranked students' prospects), warning us that ER measured as a mean can mask network sorting.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We borrow the empirical design logic: link graduates to an administrative employer-employee register (their Brazilian RAIS analogue) and define a firm's "alumni affiliation" by the share of its workers from a given university, then test whether attending raises the probability of placement at high-paying alumni-affiliated firms. For our US pipeline this maps onto using employment/earnings outcomes (College Scorecard, NSF SDR sector) to characterize ER as placement into high-paying employer networks per field — a network-based reading of ER that we can contrast with SpringRank academic prestige. The reusable object is the alumni-firm affiliation measure and the result that network access (not test-score skill) drives elite-university earnings premia.
