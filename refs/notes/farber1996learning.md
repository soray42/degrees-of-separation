# farber1996learning
**Full citation:** Farber, H.S., Gibbons, R. (1996). Learning and Wage Dynamics. The Quarterly Journal of Economics, 111(4), 1007-1047.
**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.2307/2946706 (also NBER WP 3764)
**Access level:** abstract read (NBER WP 3764 abstract); QJE full text paywalled, model details from abstract and search results

## What it contributes to THIS project
Farber-Gibbons provides the formal learning model that underlies the employer-learning leg of the project's theory chain and supplies the testable wage-dynamics restrictions we use to validate that ER is a *learning* outcome distinct from the academic signal. Its result that the effect of schooling on the *level* of wages is independent of experience, while time-invariant ability correlates that employers cannot initially see become increasingly correlated with wages as experience accumulates, is the mechanism by which behavioral Employer Reputation pulls away from credential-based Academic Reputation over a career. The martingale property of wage residuals gives a clean efficient-learning benchmark against which we can check whether SDR/Scorecard earnings trajectories are consistent with employers learning true productivity. It thus justifies treating mid/late-career outcomes as the proper ER measure when computing the field-level gap.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the three testable implications as identification restrictions: (1) schooling's coefficient on the wage *level* is constant in experience; (2) ability correlates unobserved by employers gain wage correlation as experience rises (the learning signature); (3) wage residuals follow a martingale (E[future wage | info] = current wage). Implication (2) is the operational core we port to the gap: a field whose hidden-productivity correlate becomes wage-relevant quickly is a small-gap (fast-learning) field. NLSY is the model dataset.
