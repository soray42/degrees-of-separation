# arrow1973higher
**Full citation:** Arrow, K.J. (1973). Higher Education as a Filter. Journal of Public Economics, 2(3), 193-216.
**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.1016/0047-2727(73)90013-3
**Access level:** abstract only (ScienceDirect full text returned HTTP 403; RePEc lists "no abstract available"; verified citation and standard model content via search results and secondary literature)

## What it contributes to THIS project
Arrow supplies the "filter" variant of the screening view that the project leans on to justify treating Academic Reputation as a sorting device rather than a productivity-creating one. His model posits that higher education primarily *filters/sorts* students by pre-existing ability and transmits that information to employers, rather than adding human capital — directly relevant to why a field's prestige ranking (AR) need not track realized earnings/sector outcomes (ER). This grounds the project's interpretation that a large AR-ER gap can reflect a field where the academic hierarchy filters on attributes employers do not ultimately reward. It complements Spence (signaling) and Stiglitz (screening) as the third leg of the information-economics foundation, and motivates the private-vs-social-return tension that makes the gap policy-relevant.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We do not lift a closed-form equation; we reuse Arrow's conceptual "filter" mechanism — education conveys a noisy index of pre-existing ability without raising productivity — as the rationale for modeling field-level prestige (AR) as a screening signal that may diverge from behavioral employer outcomes (ER). It supports the project's framing that the AR-ER gap measures the residual informational value (or non-value) of a field's academic filter once labor-market behavior is observed.
