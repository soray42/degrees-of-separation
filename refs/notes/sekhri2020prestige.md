# sekhri2020prestige
**Full citation:** Sekhri, Sheetal (2020). Prestige Matters: Wage Premium and Value Addition in Elite Colleges. American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 12(3), 207-225.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1257/app.20140105
**Access level:** full text (read the published AEJ:Applied PDF)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper is an empirical clean-separation of the two things our Gap conflates: prestige-based labor-market reward versus actual skill value-added. Using a regression-discontinuity at elite-college admission cutoffs in India, it shows graduates of elite public colleges earn a wage premium even though their exit/learning outcomes are no different at the cutoff — i.e., the market pays for the credential/reputation, not measured human capital added. That is the microeconomic engine of an AR-ER (or, more precisely, a prestige-vs-skill) gap: Employer Reputation can reward an institution far beyond its value-added. It directly supports our framing that signaling-heavy, credential fields should show large gaps, and it gives us a defensible RD template if we ever want institution-level causal validation of an ER premium that is decoupled from skill.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the identification design and its headline contrast: a sharp RD in admission test score at elite-college cutoffs, comparing (a) earnings/wages and (b) college exit test scores for marginal admits vs. non-admits. The reusable result is that wages jump at the cutoff while exit scores do not — operationalizing "wage premium without value addition" as evidence that reputation (ER) and skill diverge. In our project this licenses interpreting a positive ER-rank that exceeds an institution's value-added-based ranking as a signaling premium rather than human-capital production, and supplies the RD-at-cutoff blueprint for any institution-level robustness check on whether ER reflects skill or prestige.
