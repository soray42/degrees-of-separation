# rivera2015pedigree
**Full citation:** Rivera, L.A. (2015). Pedigree: How Elite Students Get Elite Jobs. Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press. (Hardcover 2015, ISBN 9780691155623; paperback 2016, ISBN 9780691169279.)
**DOI / URL:** https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691169279/pedigree
**Access level:** abstract only (publisher description, book reviews, and chapter summaries read via WebFetch/WebSearch; full text not accessed)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the qualitative anchor for the high-gap end of our spectrum: it documents, through in-depth interviews and direct observation inside top investment banks, consulting firms, and law firms, that elite employers screen first and foremost on university prestige ("pedigree") rather than on demonstrated task ability. Rivera's mechanism is precisely the divergence our AR-ER gap is built to measure: hiring decisions track institutional reputation and class-coded cultural fit instead of value-added, which is exactly why credential-heavy fields (law, finance) should show a HIGH gap between academic-prestige rank (AR) and behavioral labor-market reward (ER). The book supplies the causal microfoundation linking Spence-style signaling to actual employer behavior, motivating why our Spearman gap is non-zero in these fields and why it would be near-zero in fields (e.g., computer science) where employers can directly test task output. It also justifies treating university-prestige-driven hiring as a measurable institution-level phenomenon rather than pure individual ability sorting.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
No equation or dataset; we lift the qualitative mechanism and use it as a validation/interpretation lens. Specifically: (1) the finding that elite firms restrict hiring to a short list of "core"/elite schools and use school rank as a primary, near-binary gatekeeping filter — this predicts that in law and finance, ER (behavioral earnings/sector outcomes) will be tightly coupled to institutional prestige in a way that REWARDS credential over task fit, inflating the Gap_field where academic SpringRank prestige and ER rankings diverge; and (2) the "cultural matching" mechanism, which we cite to explain why employer reputation in these fields reflects credential/signal value rather than the task-distance-driven, ability-revealing competition we model for low-gap fields via Gathmann-Schoenberg task distance.
