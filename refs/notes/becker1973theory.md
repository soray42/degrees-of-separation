# becker1973theory
**Full citation:** Becker, Gary S. (1973). A Theory of Marriage: Part I. Journal of Political Economy, 81(4), 813-846.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1086/260084 (https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/260084)
**Access level:** abstract only (RePEc lists "no abstract"; read the full result via search snippets and the SciRP/RePEc citation records, plus the Borovickova-Shimer paper that restates Becker's condition; did not get the paywalled full text)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the foundational sorting theorem the whole AR-vs-ER framework rests on: who-matches-with-whom is governed by complementarity, so positive assortative matching (high types with high types) arises when the production function is supermodular in the two sides' traits. We borrow this to frame the academic-hiring network as a sorting market (prestigious departments hiring prestigious-trained PhDs = PAM on a single "prestige" axis), against which the labor market's ER ranking is a *different* sorting market (employers matching to value-added/task-fit). The Gap_field = 1 - Spearman(prestige_rank, ER_rank) is precisely a statement that the two markets sort on different complementarities; Becker tells us a near-zero gap (computer science) implies the trait academia sorts on is the same one employers reward, while a high gap (law, finance) implies the two production functions are supermodular in different traits. It also grounds the later refinements in the citation chain (Shimer-Smith add search frictions; Eeckhout-Kircher add identification limits and firm scale).

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
Becker's sorting result: with transferable utility and household output Z(A_m, A_w), mates sort *positively* on a trait when output is supermodular/complementary in that trait (Z_{A_m A_w} > 0, cross-partial positive) and *negatively* when traits are substitutes (cross-partial negative). We reuse this supermodularity/cross-partial criterion as the conceptual definition of "what a market sorts on," then operationalize "academia's trait" (prestige) vs "industry's trait" (task value-added/earnings) as potentially distinct arguments whose mismatch generates the measured field gap.
