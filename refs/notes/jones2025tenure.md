## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper is our citable, recent (2025) example of obtaining and using the Academic Analytics Research Center (AARC) faculty dataset — the proprietary licensed roster we need to validate and extend the AR faculty-hiring panel toward 2026. It documents the exact AARC access pathway and scale (Dataset D1: ~314,000 tenure-track/tenured scholars across 393 US PhD-granting institutions, 2011–2020), which defines the universe of institutions and fields our prestige hierarchies are built over and which our ORCID+OpenAlex pipeline must reproduce. Its lab-based vs. non-lab-based field taxonomy is directly reusable as a moderator in our gap model: post-tenure output stays high in lab fields but declines in non-lab fields (business, economics, mathematics, sociology, political science), echoing the labor/group-norm distinction in zhang2022labor and sharpening which fields we expect high AR–ER gaps in. It thus both supplies a data-access precedent and reinforces the field-heterogeneity structure underlying the task-distance hypothesis.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse (a) the AARC Dataset D1 description and access protocol ("licensed research dataset... accessible by contacting AARC directly at https://aarcresearch.com"; ~314,000 scholars, 393 institutions, 2011–2020) as our concrete plan and citation for sourcing faculty rosters; and (b) the 15-discipline lab-based vs. non-lab-based classification, which we adopt as a discipline-level moderator/control. The empirical result we carry forward: publication rates rise across the tenure track and peak just before tenure, then diverge — output remains high post-tenure in lab-based fields and declines substantially in non-lab-based fields (sample of 12,611 faculty obtaining tenure 2012–2015 across 15 disciplines). We use this trajectory heterogeneity to argue that "academic value" of a credential is field-specific, motivating why ER (labor-market outcomes) and AR (network prestige) decouple more in non-lab/credential fields.
