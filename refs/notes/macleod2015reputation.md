# macleod2015reputation
**Full citation:** MacLeod, W. Bentley, and Miguel Urquiola (2015). Reputation and School Competition. American Economic Review, 105(11), 3471-3488.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1257/aer.20130332
**Access level:** full text (read the published AER PDF)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the theoretical keystone for the "ER" side of our gap and one of the named links in our theory chain (Spence -> Altonji-Pierret -> MacLeod-Urquiola -> Gathmann-Schoenberg). The paper formalizes exactly the object we want to measure: a school's market reputation is the labor market's expectation of a graduate's productivity, and that productivity is built from student innate ability PLUS school value added (the school's causal contribution to skill). Because employers cannot directly observe ability, they price the school's reputation — which is what behavioral Employer Reputation (Scorecard earnings, SDR salary) reflects, as opposed to the faculty-hiring prestige (Academic Reputation) we rank with SpringRank. The paper's central insight — that selection-on-ability and true value-added are confounded inside any reputation signal — is precisely why an AR-ER gap can exist and why it should vary across fields with how observable/transferable skill is. It also supplies the "test prep does not raise skill" wedge, the credential-signaling logic underlying our high-gap fields (law, finance).

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the model's skill-decomposition: a graduate's skill is a function of innate ability a, study effort e, and school value added v_s (the paper states "Skill is determined by student innate ability, student study effort at school, and college value added"), while the competitive wage equals the labor market's expected skill conditional on observed signals including the school's reputation tau_s(Gamma). Because mean innate ability integrates out in equilibrium ("the aggregate effect of ability is integrated out"), the reputation premium a school commands is interpretable as a noisy mix of average selected ability and value added — the identification problem we operationalize: AR proxies one (network prestige), ER proxies the realized market belief, and Gap_field captures their divergence. We lift this ability-vs-value-added reputation decomposition as the conceptual generator of our Gap metric, not a regression coefficient.
