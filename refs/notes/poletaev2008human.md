# poletaev2008human
**Full citation:** Poletaev, M., & Robinson, C. (2008). Human Capital Specificity: Evidence from the Dictionary of Occupational Titles and Displaced Worker Surveys, 1984-2000. Journal of Labor Economics, 26(3), 387-420.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1086/588180 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jlabec/v26y2008i3p387-420.html)
**Access level:** abstract only (full text paywalled at University of Chicago Press; verified citation, abstract, and methodology summary via RePEc/EconPapers/Semantic Scholar)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the foundational empirical argument that human-capital specificity lives at the level of *skill portfolios*, not occupation or industry codes — exactly the premise our task-distance variable rests on. Poletaev and Robinson show that displaced-worker wage losses track switches in the underlying skill bundle far more tightly than switches in the nominal occupation/industry classification, which justifies our decision to measure academia-vs-industry distance over O*NET task/knowledge vectors rather than over coarse CIP or SOC labels. For our project this validates the claim that the academia-to-industry move is "cheap" (low gap) only when the skill portfolios overlap, as in computer science, and "expensive" (high gap) in credential fields where the academic skill bundle is poorly transferable. It also gives us a displacement-cost analogue for interpreting the AR-ER gap: a large gap is the field-level signature of a non-transferable academic skill portfolio. Their fluid-vs-crystallized skill distinction maps onto our intuition that general/fluid academic skills travel to industry while crystallized credential-specific skills do not.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the core methodological move: construct measures of a small number of basic skills from occupation-level descriptor data (they use four skills built from the Dictionary of Occupational Titles), characterize each job as a *skill portfolio* vector over those skills, and then compute a *distance measure between jobs* in that skill space. This is the direct precursor to our Gathmann-Schoenberg-style O*NET CIP->SOC task-distance metric: portfolio vector per node, distance between an academic node and an industry node. We also lift their headline identification result as a benchmark and citation anchor — wage loss is associated with the magnitude of the skill-portfolio change, not the occupation/industry code switch — to motivate why the AR-ER gap should be predicted by task distance rather than by nominal field labels.
