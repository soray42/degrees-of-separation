# qs2025critical
**Full citation:** Badiuzzaman, MD (2025). Unpacking the metrics: a critical analysis of the 2025 QS World University Rankings using Australian university data. Frontiers in Education, 10, 1619897.
**DOI / URL:** https://doi.org/10.3389/feduc.2025.1619897
**Access level:** full text (open access)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper provides the empirical warning that, *inside the QS ranking system*, Academic Reputation and Employer Reputation are nearly redundant — exactly the collapse our project argues is an artifact of using survey-perception ER instead of behavioral ER. Badiuzzaman finds AR and ER are "nearly collinear" (r = 0.98) and each almost perfectly tracks the overall QS score, which means QS's ER carries little information independent of AR; our project's entire contribution rests on the claim that *behavioral* ER (Scorecard earnings, SDR salary/sector) breaks this collinearity and reveals a real, field-varying gap. So this is our motivating null: if perception-ER and AR are interchangeable, then any nonzero, field-structured Gap we recover must come from the behavioral measurement, not from re-labeling. It also documents the systemic bias toward large research-intensive institutions, supporting our choice to rank *within field* and to anchor prestige on a hiring-network SpringRank score rather than on size-confounded reputation surveys.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
The concrete results we cite are the Pearson correlations with overall QS score on 2025 Australian data (n = 31): Academic Reputation r(29) = 0.98 (p < 0.001), Employer Reputation r(29) = 0.97 (p < 0.001), Employment Outcomes r(29) = 0.94 (p < 0.001), Citations per Faculty r(29) = 0.79 (p < 0.001); and the central AR-ER collinearity r(29) = 0.98 (p < 0.001) flagged as "potential redundancy." We reuse this AR-ER correlation as the survey-perception baseline against which we contrast our behavioral Gap_field = 1 - Spearman(prestige_rank, ER_rank): where QS's perception-ER yields ~0.98 with AR, our behavioral ER should yield materially lower, field-dependent rank correlations (low gap for CS, high gap for law/finance). Note the finding is associational (Pearson on aggregate scores), not an identification design.
