# boyack2014creation
**Full citation:** Boyack, K. W., & Klavans, R. (2014). Creation of a Highly Detailed, Dynamic, Global Model and Map of Science. *Journal of the Association for Information Science and Technology*, 65(4), 670–685.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1002/asi.22990
**Access level:** abstract only (Wiley/ScholarsPortal/akjournals pages returned 403/302/IP-block; abstract read via RePEc/IDEAS and corroborating search results; RePEc page itself carried "no abstract," details confirmed from Wiley listing and search snippets)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the highest-resolution version of the science-map covariate and the one that best matches a panel design extended to 2026. Where the 2005 and 2009 papers map science at the journal/discipline level, this paper builds an **article-level, dynamic** model (16 years, ~20 million articles), so it can supply finer-grained and time-varying field positions to align with the proposal's panel and with the ORCID+OpenAlex faculty-hiring pipeline. The "dynamic" aspect lets us address whether a field's applied/interdisciplinary position (and hence the predicted gap) shifts over the panel window rather than being fixed. Its emphasis on identifying emerging vs. persisting and innovative areas gives an additional lens on which fields are tightly coupled to industry application — directly relevant to the academia-vs-industry task-distance mechanism behind the AR–ER gap.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
The reusable artifact is the **co-citation-based article-level clustering pipeline**: ~20 million articles over 16 years grouped via direct-/co-citation clustering into a fine-grained set of research topics, then laid out as a global map, with time stamps enabling dynamic tracking (emerging vs. persisting topics). We lift the topic-clustering/layout approach to assign each field (and CIP) a fine-grained, optionally year-specific science-map position, used as the continuous "interdisciplinary/applied" covariate and as an alternative-clustering robustness check against the Wapman 8-domain taxonomy.
