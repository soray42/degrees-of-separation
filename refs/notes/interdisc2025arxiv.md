# interdisc2025arxiv
**Full citation:** Zheng, X., Peng, A., Hong, X., Sugimoto, C. R., & Ni, C. (2025). Interdisciplinary PhDs face barriers to top university placement within their disciplines. arXiv preprint arXiv:2503.21912 (v2, Nov 2025).
**DOI / URL:** arXiv:2503.21912 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21912)
**Access level:** open-access preprint — read abstract and methodology details (authors, sample, data sources, interdisciplinarity construction) via arXiv abstract page and corroborating searches; the full PDF text would not extract cleanly, so quantitative method internals are summarized from the abstract and secondary descriptions.

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is our concrete template for the modern faculty-hiring data pipeline that extends the AR panel to ~2025–2026: it links large-scale faculty-roster data (Academic Analytics-style rosters) with ProQuest Dissertations to recover each faculty member's PhD field, institution, and placement institution at scale (32,977 tenure-track faculty, PhDs after 2005, placed at 355 US universities). That AARC-roster-plus-ProQuest linkage is exactly the join we need to attach a CIP-coded PhD field to each placement edge, which is the key for both our prestige (AR) network construction by field and the CIP-based task-distance join. Its substantive finding — top universities preferentially hire less interdisciplinary PhDs, disadvantaging women, despite interdisciplinary hires being more productive — is a placement-selection confound we must net out when reading prestige ranks as "reputation." It also gives us a worked, recent (2025) example of building per-field placement networks on the same population we replicate with the ORCID+OpenAlex pipeline.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the data-linkage design: faculty employment/placement rosters joined to ProQuest Dissertations & Theses to obtain each person's doctoral field and dissertation content, with disciplinary/topic classification built from dissertation titles (the abstract/secondary sources describe ~444 distinct subject topics used as features). This is the algorithmic step we replicate for assigning a CIP/field label to each hiring edge so that prestige hierarchies can be cut per field and joined to O*NET CIP→SOC task vectors. We do NOT lift a specific regression coefficient (the paper is paywalled-at-text level for us); we lift the dataset construction and the qualitative placement-bias result (top-ranked hires are less interdisciplinary; effect penalizes women) as a control/robustness consideration.
