## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper is a direct precedent for turning institution-level quality indicators into an earnings-anchored quality index, which is structurally the same construction we use to build Academic/Employer reputation measures from observable signals. It builds a cross-country higher-education-quality index by weighting institution-level indicators (faculty-to-student ratio, global university rankings) by their estimated effect on the earnings of graduates working abroad — an "outcome-weighted reputation" recipe we can mirror when we weight reputation components by their labor-market payoff. For our US-only design it is mostly a methodological/external-validity analogue (it operates at the country level, not the field-within-country level we need), but it validates the logic that quality/reputation should be calibrated against realized earnings. It also supplies an instrument idea (proximity to global academic hubs) for the endogeneity of quality, relevant if we ever need exogenous variation in prestige.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the index-construction recipe: regress overseas-graduate earnings on institution-level indicators (faculty-student ratio, global rankings) to obtain weights, then aggregate weighted institutional characteristics into a quality index (here a country-level index covering ~98 countries). The transplantable methodological element is "weight observable reputation/quality indicators by their estimated effect on graduate earnings" — the earnings-anchored weighting we can apply within field to combine ER signals. The IV strategy — geographic distance/proximity to global academic hubs (the world's top universities) — is a reusable identification device for the endogeneity of education quality in any downstream regression of outcomes on reputation.
