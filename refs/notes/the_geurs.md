# the_geurs
**Full citation:** Emerging / Times Higher Education (2025). Global Employability University Ranking and Survey (GEURS) — Methodology. Emerging, Paris.
**DOI / URL:** https://www.emerging.fr/geurs/methodology
**Access level:** full text (public methodology page)

## What it contributes to THIS project
GEURS is the purest existing operationalization of pure Employer Reputation (ER) — it ranks universities *solely* on what graduate employers believe, with zero academic-prestige or research input — making it the cleanest external benchmark for the ER side of our gap. Where QS blends AR and ER, GEURS isolates ER, so it is the construct our behavioral ER measures (Scorecard earnings, SDR salary/sector) try to approximate without relying on employer perception surveys. Its "drivers" framework is directly relevant to our central hypothesis: by attaching each employer vote to up-to-two drivers (work expertise, graduate skills, specialization, academic performance, etc.), GEURS already decomposes *why* employers value a university, which parallels our MacLeod-style reputation = ability + value-added decomposition and the Gathmann-Schoenberg task content that we argue drives the AR-ER gap. We use GEURS as a survey-perception ceiling against which to show that behavioral ER both correlates with and diverges from stated employer reputation, and as evidence that ER is a measurable, separable axis from AR.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse GEURS's *vote-and-driver design* as a template for interpreting ER, plus its 2025 scale parameters as reference: ~12,350 screened international employer/operational-manager respondents casting ~119,967 validated votes, each respondent selecting 10-15 universities from a curated list of ~1,000 institutions across 100+ countries, with each vote justified by up to two of seven drivers (Work Expertise, Graduate Skills, Specialization, Social Impact & Leadership, Academic Performance, Internationality, Reputation). The published artifact is the GEURS Top 250 (institutions with the highest count of validated votes; raw vote counts, no normalization/weighting). The methodologically load-bearing element we lift is the driver-attribution mechanism — votes mapped to skill/specialization drivers — which we treat as a perception-side analog of the task-content decomposition our project builds from O*NET CIP->SOC vectors.
