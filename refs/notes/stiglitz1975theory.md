# stiglitz1975theory
**Full citation:** Stiglitz, J.E. (1975). The Theory of "Screening," Education, and the Distribution of Income. The American Economic Review, 65(3), 283-300.
**DOI / URL:** https://www.jstor.org/stable/1804834
**Access level:** abstract only (JSTOR/AEA full text restricted; RePEc/EconPapers lists no abstract; verified citation and standard model summary via search results, Cowles Foundation listing, and secondary literature)

## What it contributes to THIS project
Stiglitz completes the screening-theory foundation by analyzing education as a mechanism that *identifies* (screens) heterogeneous ability and showing how this reshapes the income distribution — connecting the project's information-economics framing to distributional outcomes, which is what the Employer Reputation side measures (Scorecard earnings, SDR salaries). His emphasis that screening can be privately valuable yet socially over- or mis-invested supports the project's claim that a field's academic prestige ordering (AR) can diverge from the labor market's valuation (ER). Together with Spence and Arrow, it justifies treating AR as a screening/credential signal whose payoff is field-specific. This is the third pillar of the Spence-Arrow-Stiglitz information block underpinning the gap hypothesis.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the conceptual screening result rather than a formula: education sorts/identifies ability and the returns to that screening map into the income distribution, so the wedge between credential-based rankings and realized earnings is theoretically expected and field-dependent. This underwrites the project's operationalization of Gap_field = 1 - Spearman(prestige_rank, ER_rank) as the empirical residual between a field's screening signal (AR) and its market valuation (ER).
