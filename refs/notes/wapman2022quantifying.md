# wapman2022quantifying
**Full citation:** Wapman, K. H., Zhang, S., Clauset, A., & Larremore, D. B. (2022). Quantifying hierarchy and dynamics in US faculty hiring and retention. *Nature*, 610(7930), 120–127.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1038/s41586-022-05222-x — open-access full text at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9534765/ (Author Correction: 10.1038/s41586-023-06379-9)
**Access level:** full text (read via PMC open-access HTML)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the BENCHMARK against which we validate our 2026 ORCID+OpenAlex faculty-hiring pipeline, and it is the **Tier-0 source of Academic Reputation (AR)**. Our AR = field-specific SpringRank prestige per institution comes directly from this paper's hiring network: a directed edge runs from the PhD-granting institution to the institution that hired the faculty member, and SpringRank embeds each institution as a scalar prestige score within each field. The paper's 107-field / 8-domain taxonomy is exactly the granularity at which we compute Gap_field = 1 − Spearman(prestige_rank, ER_rank). Because the AARC field labels here are not natively CIP codes, this reference also defines the crosswalk problem we must solve (AARC field → CIP) so that CIP can serve as our join key to Scorecard/SDR/O*NET. Computer Science (a "Formal Sciences"/computing field here) is the field we hypothesize as the near-zero-gap extreme, so its prestige ranks from this paper are load-bearing. The paper's documented inequalities (a small minority of universities produce most faculty) characterize the AR distribution whose mismatch with behavioral labor-market ER we are measuring.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
- **Coverage we lift:** tenured/tenure-track faculty employed 2011–2020 at **368 PhD-granting US universities**; **n = 295,089 faculty** in **10,612 departments**; **107 fields** nested in **8 domains**. Source data: a US faculty census from the **Academic Analytics Research Center (AARC)** under a data-use agreement (the public release on Zenodo is deidentified — see `wapman2022data`).
- **8 domains** (as named/referenced in the paper, used as our domain-level aggregation): Natural Sciences; Engineering; Social Sciences; Humanities; Medicine and Health; Mathematics and Computing; Education; and the Formal/Applied-Sciences grouping (with Public Administration & Policy and Journalism/Media/Communications noted as additional fields excluded from some domain-level analyses). Fields are NOT mutually exclusive — ~23% of faculty are assigned to multiple fields, a caveat we carry when joining a faculty member to a single CIP.
- **Prestige construction (what we read as AR):** "we used the **SpringRank algorithm without regularization**, producing a scalar embedding of each network's nodes." For each TaxonomyLevel (Academia / Domain / Field) the directed hiring network (edge i→j = institution i produced a PhD now employed by j) is solved by SpringRank (De Bacco, Larremore & Moore 2018: each directed edge → an oriented spring with displacement s_i − s_j; ranks s minimize total spring energy via a sparse linear system). Per-field prestige is computed separately, which is precisely the within-field prestige_rank we correlate against ER.
- **Concrete column we use as `SpringRank_prestige` in Tier 0:** in `ranks.csv` of the companion data, **`PrestigeRank`** (Float, SpringRank scaled to 0–1 where 0 = highest prestige, 1 = lowest), filtered to `TaxonomyLevel == "Field"` and the relevant `TaxonomyValue` (e.g. "Computer Science"). `OrdinalPrestigeRank` (Integer) is the ordinal version we feed into Spearman; `ProductionRank` is a separate faculty-count rank we do NOT use for AR.
