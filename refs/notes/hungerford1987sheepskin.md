## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the foundational empirical demonstration that the labor market rewards *credential completion* discontinuously, over and above the continuous human-capital return to years of schooling — the canonical "sheepskin" / signaling result that underpins our Spence-to-Altonji-Pierret theory chain. For our project it grounds the claim that ER (employer reputation, measured behaviorally through earnings) embeds a degree-completion premium that is about the credential as a signal, not about marginal task skill, which is exactly the wedge that can pull ER rank away from academic-prestige (AR) rank within a field. It motivates why credential fields (law, finance) — where the diploma itself is the gate — should exhibit a large AR-ER gap, while fields where employers learn ability directly should not. It is the historical precedent for the identification logic we invoke when interpreting discontinuities/level-shifts in earnings tied to degree attainment in the College Scorecard and NSF SDR data.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
The reusable object is the identification design: augment a continuous (Mincer-type) log-earnings regression on years of schooling with **dummy variables marking degree-completion years** (notably 12 = high-school diploma and 16 = bachelor's degree), i.e. fit a spline/piecewise-linear function of years of schooling and test whether the credential dummies produce a statistically significant *discontinuous jump* in earnings at those thresholds. The paper's reported result (as characterized in the replication literature) is that such jumps are significant — i.e., genuine "sheepskin effects" exist — establishing that earnings are not a smooth function of years of education. We reuse this dummy-at-degree-year specification as the template for separating a credential/signaling premium from continuous skill returns when we decompose ER, and as the precedent for treating degree-level (PhD via SDR vs. undergrad via Scorecard) as a discrete signal rather than a continuous control.
