# clauset2015systematic
**Full citation:** Clauset, A., Arbesman, S., & Larremore, D. B. (2015). Systematic inequality and hierarchy in faculty hiring networks. *Science Advances, 1*(1), e1400005.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1126/sciadv.1400005 (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.1400005); companion data/code: https://aaronclauset.github.io/facultyhiring/
**Access level:** abstract only for the journal page (HTTP 403 paywall); full methods read via the open-access supplementary materials PDF (equation 1 and definitions quoted directly) and the author's companion site.

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the foundational paper for the Academic Reputation (AR) side of the gap. AR in this project is defined as faculty-hiring-network prestige per field, and this paper is the canonical source of the minimum-violation-ranking (MVR) prestige extraction that we (and the Wapman et al. 2022 validation target) rely on. It demonstrates the core empirical claim our gap presupposes — that doctoral-institution prestige is a steep, near-universal hierarchy that predicts placement better than US News rank — establishing prestige_rank as a meaningful within-field ordering of institutions. Its three-discipline design (computer science, business, history) directly motivates our field-contrast hypothesis: business/credential fields vs. CS as opposite ends of the prestige-vs-outcome relationship. We use its CS prestige hierarchy as the near-zero-gap reference case and its ranking machinery as the AR rank that we Spearman-correlate against Employer Reputation (ER).

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the MVR objective (supplement eq. 1): given a directed weighted hiring network A with permutation π (π_u = 1 is highest-ranked), the prestige order maximizes the net weight of unviolated edges, S[π(A)] = Σ_{uv} A_{uv} · sign[π(v) − π(u)], found by zero-temperature MCMC sampling over all MVRs, with hierarchy strength ρ = fraction of edges pointing down the ranking (rate at which faculty place no higher than their doctoral institution). Sample: ~18,924 tenure-track faculty (16,316 with in-sample doctorate) across CS (4,388 faculty / 205 institutions), business (7,856 / 112), history (4,072 / 144). We lift the per-field MVR prestige vector (and its consensus/averaged-rank variant from Fig. S1) as the AR rank input.
