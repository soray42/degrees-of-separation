## What it contributes to THIS project
Manuel (2024) supplies the field-of-study side of our task-distance machinery and a clean behavioral earnings premium for *alignment* between a degree's field and the knowledge content of the job actually held. This is structurally identical to our employer-reputation (ER) construction: the labor market rewards graduates whose occupation's knowledge requirements match the knowledge their field trains for, which is exactly the field-to-occupation overlap we compute from O*NET to build CIP->SOC distance. It directly motivates our central hypothesis — fields where the academic-to-industry knowledge overlap is high (low distance) convert their credential into market value, while low-alignment fields do not — and gives us a published behavioral magnitude (a positive, economically meaningful alignment premium) to calibrate the ER signal against the SpringRank academic-reputation signal. Because his identification uses field-of-study and occupation fixed effects, it isolates the *within-field* value of being well-aligned, which is the same conditioning we need so that the AR-ER gap reflects alignment rather than field-level pay differences. It is the closest published analogue to the Spence-signaling-to-employer-learning chain operationalized via an O*NET knowledge-vector alignment score.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the *alignment score* itself: a similarity measure between the knowledge-requirement vector of an individual's actual occupation and the knowledge-requirement vectors of the occupations that their field of study trains people for. This is the direct template for our CIP->SOC knowledge/task-vector distance (we invert it: distance = 1 - alignment) and it confirms occupation knowledge descriptors (O*NET-style) as the right vector space for the join. We also lift the headline result as a benchmark: controlling for field-of-study and occupation fixed effects, a one-standard-deviation improvement in knowledge alignment yields an earnings premium of approximately 4.3% (Canadian Census bachelor's-degree holders). We use both the fixed-effects specification (field FE + occupation FE) and that ~4.3%/SD magnitude as the reference point for the slope of the ER-vs-alignment relationship at the undergraduate (Scorecard) level.
