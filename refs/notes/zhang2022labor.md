# zhang2022labor
**Full citation:** Zhang, S., Wapman, K. H., Larremore, D. B., & Clauset, A. (2022). Labor advantages drive the greater productivity of faculty at elite universities. *Science Advances, 8*(46), eabq7056.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1126/sciadv.abq7056 (https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abq7056); data/code: https://zenodo.org/records/7126263 (Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.7126263)
**Access level:** abstract only for the journal page (paywall); full abstract, dataset description, and Zenodo deposit confirmed via WebSearch.

## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper supplies a large, linked faculty dataset that couples the same prestige hierarchy used for AR with productivity and — critically for us — federal survey data, anchoring the bridge between the AR network and behavioral outcomes. It combines employment, publication, and federal survey records for 78,802 tenure-track faculty at 262 PhD-granting US institutions, the same population frame from which we draw prestige ranks, so it lets us connect institution-level AR to individual-level PhD outcomes that feed the NSF SDR (Survey of Doctorate Recipients) ER measure. Its central causal finding — that prestige raises productivity mainly through labor/group-size availability, concentrated in lab-collaboration disciplines — is a confound we must control: a field where prestige buys productivity (lab fields) behaves differently from credential fields (law, finance), reinforcing our task-distance mechanism. It also serves, alongside Wapman et al. 2022, as a validation anchor for our ORCID+OpenAlex hiring pipeline since it uses the same faculty roster and prestige construction.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the Zenodo deposit (10.5281/zenodo.7126263): the linked roster of 78,802 tenure-track faculty across 262 PhD-granting institutions with institution prestige, publication counts, and federal-survey-derived attributes, plus the field classification distinguishing group-collaboration ("lab") disciplines from individual-scholarship ones. Concretely we lift (a) the institution-prestige column as a cross-check on our MVR AR rank, and (b) the lab-vs-nonlab discipline flag as a moderator/control variable when modeling the AR–ER gap against task distance, since their result shows the prestige→productivity environmental effect operates through funded graduate/postdoc labor and is strongest under group-collaboration norms.
