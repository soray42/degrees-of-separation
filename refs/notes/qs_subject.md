**DOI / URL:** https://www.topuniversities.com/subject-rankings/methodology

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the canonical real-world instantiation of the AR-vs-ER distinction that our entire project re-grounds in behavioral data. QS by Subject ranks programs on five indicators in which the two reputation channels are exactly our two constructs: an Academic Reputation (AR) indicator built from academic peer nomination surveys, and an Employer Reputation (ER) indicator built from graduate-employer surveys ("which institutions produce the best graduates, and from which disciplines do you prefer to recruit"). Crucially, QS computes both AR and ER *within subject* (comparing subject-level reputation against faculty-area reputation so specialist institutions are not penalized), which is precisely the within-field unit at which we define Gap_field. We use QS as the conceptual reference point and as a *survey-based* contrast: QS's ER is employers' stated perception, whereas our project deliberately replaces survey-perception ER with *behavioral* labor-market outcomes (Scorecard earnings, SDR salary/sector), so the QS AR-ER pair is the strawman our behavioral redefinition improves upon. It also supplies the "subjects where AR and ER diverge" framing that motivates the task-distance hypothesis.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the *indicator architecture*, not a formula: the five QS-by-Subject indicators — (1) Academic Reputation (peer survey, ~7,000 institutions evaluated annually), (2) Employer Reputation (graduate-employer survey, discipline-specific recruiter preferences), (3) Citations per Paper (Scopus, with per-subject minimum-publication thresholds), (4) H-index, (5) International Research Network (IRN). Two operational details we lift directly: (a) QS's within-subject normalization of reputation against the relevant faculty area — the analog of our within-field ranking before computing Spearman; and (b) the eligibility rule that AR + ER scores must sum to >= 5, which we note as QS's own evidence that AR and ER are the two load-bearing axes. Weighting per indicator varies by subject and is not disclosed on the public page (support-portal table was inaccessible); we therefore cite the indicator set, not specific percentage weights.
