# klavans2009toward
**Full citation:** Klavans, R., & Boyack, K. W. (2009). Toward a Consensus Map of Science. *Journal of the American Society for Information Science and Technology*, 60(3), 455–476.
**DOI / URL:** 10.1002/asi.20991
**Access level:** abstract only (Wiley page returned 402; abstract, method, and full disciplinary ordering read via ScirP reference page and corroborating search results)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper gives the proposal a *defensible, consensus* version of the field-structure covariate rather than one arbitrary map. The proposal proposes Boyack–Klavans field proximity as an alternative clustering and as a continuous applied/interdisciplinary axis; the consensus map supplies a single circular ordering of disciplines that is robust across 20 input maps, which is exactly what we want when choosing field clusters that reviewers will not contest. The circular order it reports — math, physics, physical chemistry, engineering, chemistry, earth sciences, biology, biochemistry, infectious diseases, medicine, health services, brain research, psychology, humanities, social sciences, computer science — places computer science adjacent to mathematics and far from the credentialing social/professional fields, which dovetails with the central hypothesis that CS is the near-zero-gap extreme and law/finance are high-gap. The map's adjacency structure can be turned into a continuous field-distance variable correlating with the AR–ER gap.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
The reusable method is the **consensus-edge construction**: take many independently produced science maps and retain only edges (discipline adjacencies) that appear in at least half of the input maps, yielding a robust circular consensus map; pruning the lowest-weighted consensus edges then collapses the circle into a hierarchy from mathematics to social sciences, and a one-dimensional Riemannian projection is offered. We reuse (a) the consensus-edge filtering idea to make our field-clustering robust across alternative maps, and (b) the published circular/1-D ordering of disciplines as the field position variable to test against the gap (and to justify CS's placement next to mathematics).
