# chetty2023diversifying
**Full citation:** Chetty, Raj, David J. Deming, and John N. Friedman (2023). Diversifying Society's Leaders? The Determinants and Causal Effects of Admission to Highly Selective Private Colleges. NBER Working Paper No. 31492 (rev. August 2025). (Published in Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2026, 141(1).)
**DOI / URL:** 10.3386/w31492 (https://www.nber.org/papers/w31492)
**Access level:** abstract only (read NBER abstract page; full PDF not parsed)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This paper is the clearest demonstration that elite-college Employer Reputation lives in the tails and in prestige-sorting, not in mean earnings — which is decisive for how we measure ER. It finds that attending an Ivy-Plus college (vs. a flagship public) barely moves average earnings but sharply raises the chance of reaching the top 1% of the earnings distribution and of working at a "prestigious firm," exactly the labor-market prestige channel our ER should capture and that diverges from skill value-added. For our gap, this means ER built only on median Scorecard earnings could understate elite-institution reputation, so we should also weight top-tail and elite-employer placement (mirrored in SDR sector). It also separates admission determinants (legacy, athletics, non-academic ratings) that predict prestige access but not productivity — a near-textbook signaling-vs-ability wedge feeding the AR-ER gap.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the outcome definitions and magnitudes: attending an Ivy-Plus college "increases students' chances of reaching the top 1% of the earnings distribution by 50%" and "almost triples their chances of working at a prestigious firm," estimated by linking college-specific admissions records to income-tax records and test scores, using waitlist/admissions variation for the causal effect. The transplantable object is the menu of prestige-tail outcomes — top-1% earnings probability and elite-firm placement probability — which we adopt as ER components (alongside median earnings) so that Employer Reputation reflects elite-employer access, not just central-tendency pay, when ranked against SpringRank academic prestige.
