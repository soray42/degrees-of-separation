# altonji1998employer
**Full citation:** Altonji, J.G., Pierret, C.R. (1996/1998). Employer Learning and the Signaling Value of Education. NBER Working Paper No. 5438 (Jan 1996); published version in P. de Gijsel, H. Wolff & D. Sadowski (eds.), Internal Labour Markets, Incentives and Employment, Macmillan/St. Martin's Press.
**DOI / URL:** https://www.nber.org/papers/w5438 (DOI 10.3386/w5438)
**Access level:** abstract only (NBER working-paper abstract read; full text paywalled)

## What it contributes to THIS project
This is the earlier, foundational statement of the Altonji-Pierret employer-learning framework and the one that names the "signaling value of education" explicitly — the precise quantity the AR-ER gap is trying to detect at the field level. It introduces the wage equation containing the experience interaction with a hard-to-observe productivity variable and the experience interaction with easily observed education, and uses the time-path of those coefficients to estimate the *rate at which employers learn* worker productivity. For this project it supplies the conceptual measure: if academia's prestige signal has lasting signaling value (slow learning), the gap persists; if employer learning is fast, ER decouples from AR. It pairs with altonji2001employer as the working-paper origin of the identification we adapt to SDR and Scorecard cohorts.

## Specific equation / result / dataset we reuse
We reuse the preliminary employer-learning specification: a wage equation with experience-interacted terms for (a) a hard-to-observe correlate of productivity and (b) easily observed schooling, where the evolving coefficient on the unobservable indexes the speed of employer learning. We treat this "learning-rate" parameterization as the template for interpreting how fast each field's labor market overwrites the academic signal — operationally, the field-level slope of ER on prestige as cohorts age, estimated from NLSY-style logic ported to NSF SDR.
