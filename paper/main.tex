\begin{abstract}
Academic prestige and labour-market placement are two orderings of the same human capital
(institution $\times$ field): where the \emph{academy} ranks a program (academic reputation, AR, realised in the
faculty-hiring network) and where the \emph{labour market} places its graduates (revealed placement, proxied by
earnings). These are not two reputations; they are a \emph{perception} and a \emph{realised outcome}, and the
wedge between them is informative. We measure that wedge --- the academic-reputation--placement gap,
$\mathrm{gap}_f = 1 - \mathrm{Spearman}(\text{field prestige},\,\text{placement})$ within field across
institutions --- using a continuous SpringRank reconstruction of the US faculty-hiring network
\citep{wapman2022quantifying, debacco2018physical} and College Scorecard field-of-study earnings, cross-validated
against Census PSEO \citep{scorecard, pseo}. Four findings stand. \textbf{(1) Measurement:} the gap is a
\emph{measurable, reliable} object, not an artifact (only 15\% of fields sit inside a perfect-agreement-plus-noise
null, 65\% above; the artifact channel explains $R^2=0.20$). \textbf{(2) Structure:} it is
\emph{discipline-structured} --- a precision-weighted multilevel decomposition puts $\sim$30\% of cross-field gap
variance between broad discipline areas (ICC $=0.30$; measurement-corrected $0.45$; $\eta^2=0.50$), with an
integrated end (Computing, Mathematics/Statistics, Economics-adjacent social science, Engineering) and a
decoupled end (Health, Arts, the natural sciences). \textbf{(3) Proxy validation:} earnings is a
\emph{validated-but-incomplete} placement proxy --- PSEO and Scorecard rank institution pay near-identically
(Spearman $+0.94$) and the field gap replicates ($+0.68$), and salary tracks non-wage placement (occupational
prestige, vertical match) at $+0.65$ to $+0.84$ across horizons and sources. \textbf{(4) Mechanism:} a single
external channel cleanly moves the gap --- occupational \emph{licensing} ($b=+0.66$) --- and we identify its
mechanism: in regulated, locally-employed fields, pay is set by \emph{setting} (state, employer, local pay scale),
so school prestige adds essentially zero incremental wage-predictive power beyond geography
($\mathrm{corr}(\text{licensure},\,\text{incremental prestige }R^2)=-0.59$; Nursing $\approx 0$). The practical
implication is direct: because the prestige$\leftrightarrow$placement structure is \emph{field-specific}, a
field-agnostic overall ranking (QS, US News overall) systematically mis-prices programs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and stakes}\label{sec:intro}
Students, employers, and policy-makers read institutional prestige as a summary of educational quality, and the
dominant public signals --- overall university rankings (QS, US News overall) --- are \emph{field-agnostic}: a
single ``brand'' is made to stand for the quality of every program. \textbf{If the way academic prestige maps onto
labour-market placement is itself field-specific, a field-agnostic ranking systematically mis-prices programs} ---
and the evidence that rankings move student and institutional behaviour \citep{luca2013salience} makes that a
welfare-relevant error. This paper measures the object such a ranking hides.

The object is a wedge between two orderings of the same institution-by-field human capital. The \emph{academic}
ordering is where the academy places an institution, realised in who hires whose PhDs: the faculty-hiring network
induces a prestige hierarchy \citep{wapman2022quantifying}. The \emph{placement} ordering is where the labour
market puts the institution's graduates, realised in earnings. Crucially these are \emph{not} two measurements of
the same ``reputation.'' One is a \emph{perception} held inside the academy; the other is a \emph{realised
outcome} in the labour market. We read their disagreement, within a field across institutions, as the
academic-reputation--placement gap, and frame it through signalling \citep{spence1973job} and employer-learning
theory \citep{altonji2001employer}, in which the weight on a credential signal decays as employers observe
productivity, and reputation raises pay precisely where employer information is scarce \citep{macleod2017big}.

Four findings carry the paper, each stated as a result: the gap is (1) measurable and reliable; (2)
discipline-structured; (3) robust to an independent earnings source and a faithful, if incomplete, reading of
revealed placement; and (4) moved by one clean mechanism --- occupational licensing --- whose operation we
identify. Section~\ref{sec:data} establishes the measurement, Section~\ref{sec:structure} the discipline
structure, Section~\ref{sec:proxy} the proxy validation and the licensing mechanism, Section~\ref{sec:robust} the
robustness, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} the ranking-critique implication and the forward frontier;
Section~\ref{sec:limits} states the limitations. Supporting analyses --- an organising two-signal model, three
ruled-out alternative explanations, and a dynamic-revaluation pilot --- are in the Supplementary Information.

\section{Data and the wedge: a measurable object}\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Academic reputation (AR).} We use the public US faculty-hiring network of
\citet{wapman2022quantifying}. Because the licensing analysis and the applied ranking need meaningful score
\emph{differences}, not just ordinal ranks, we recompute the continuous SpringRank score
\citep{debacco2018physical} on the public field-level edge subgraphs. A from-scratch reconstruction reproduces the
published ranks at a data ceiling ($\rho\approx0.77$; the loss reflects the lossiness of the public aggregated
edges), and an independent ORCID-derived rebuild of the hiring network reproduces Wapman's academia
ranks at Spearman $0.74$ --- certifying the method against an independent network.

\paragraph{Revealed placement (the earnings proxy).} The labour market does not publish a placement rank; what it
publishes is the realised \emph{price}, the wage. We use College Scorecard Field-of-Study median earnings
(bachelor's, four-year-after-completion) \citep{scorecard} as the proxy for revealed placement, and validate it as
a proxy in Section~\ref{sec:proxy} (against an independent earnings source and against non-wage placement
measures). Scorecard covers Title-IV graduates; we cross-validate against Census PSEO (UI-wage-based, real 2023
dollars) \citep{pseo}.

\paragraph{Join and coverage.} The Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) is the join key. Scorecard
covers $\sim$69\% of Wapman institutions with even coverage across prestige quartiles
(74.2/64.5/71.7/65.6\% top to bottom), so the gap is not a coverage artifact; PSEO covers $\sim$29\% and is
top-truncated (21.5\% of the top prestige quartile), so PSEO is used for cross-validation, not as the primary
source. PSEO doctoral and master's cells are fully disclosure-suppressed at the 4-digit-CIP grain
(18{,}442 doctoral rows, 0 released), which is why the analysis is at the bachelor's level (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}).

\paragraph{The gap, and that it is real.} For field $f$ with institutions $i$,
\[
\mathrm{gap}_f = 1 - \mathrm{Spearman}_i\!\left(\text{prestige}_{i,f},\ \text{placement}_{i,f}\right).
\]
The gap is a \emph{rank} statistic within field, so it is insensitive to the level differences that dominate raw
earnings (engineering out-earns education) and asks only whether, among the institutions that teach $f$, the
prestige ordering and the placement ordering coincide; $0$ is perfect agreement, larger is more decoupled. A
diagnostic battery (null-model, bootstrap, residualization, restriction) establishes that the gap is \emph{real
signal}: only 15\% of fields' observed gaps fall inside a perfect-agreement-plus-noise null band while 65\% sit
above it, and a global-artifact channel explains only $R^2=0.20$ of cross-field variance. A per-field reliability
filter (signal fraction $\ge0.5$ and bootstrap CI excluding the null band) admits well-measured fields ---
Computer Science prestige--earnings correlation $0.71\,[0.62,0.78]$, Statistics $0.75\,[0.52,0.87]$, the genuine
high-gap Biology $0.37\,[0.22,0.52]$ ($N=138$) --- and screens a noise-dominated low-earnings-signal cluster.
The gap is a measurable, reliable object; everything below runs on the screened field universe.

\section{The gap is structured by discipline}\label{sec:structure}
The central structural result is that the gap is primarily a property of \emph{which discipline a field belongs
to}. A precision-weighted multilevel meta-model
$\mathrm{gap}_f \sim 1 + (1\,|\,\text{CIP-2})$
(REML; marginal covariance $V=\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{SE}^2)+\tau^2\cdot\text{same-parent}$), fit on $n=48$ reliable
fine fields across 18 CIP-2 clusters, gives between-cluster variance $\tau^2=0.0231$ and
\[
\mathrm{ICC} = \frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2+\overline{\text{within-cluster var}}} = 0.30,
\]
i.e.\ $\sim$30\% of cross-field gap variance is \emph{between} broad discipline areas (one-way $\eta^2=0.50$).
Correcting the within-cluster variance for per-field sampling noise raises this to $0.45$, and expanding the field
universe (Section~\ref{sec:robust}) to $0.57$: noise-correction and a fuller taxonomy both \emph{strengthen} the
between-discipline share.

\paragraph{The integrated--decoupled typology.} The shrunk, partial-pooled CIP-2 cluster means
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cluster}) order from integrated --- Computer/Info $0.348$, Mathematics \& Statistics $0.352$, Social
Science $0.421$, Engineering $0.432$ --- to decoupled --- Health $0.753$, Arts $0.746$, Natural Resources $0.730$,
Biological Sciences $0.678$, Physical Sciences $0.661$. In the integrated disciplines academic prestige and
labour-market placement rank institutions alike; in the decoupled disciplines they part ways. The map of every
reliable field (Fig.~\ref{fig:gapmap}) is the object a field-agnostic ranking collapses.

\section{Salary as revealed placement, and the licensing mechanism}\label{sec:proxy}
\subsection{Earnings is a validated --- if incomplete --- placement proxy}\label{sec:proxyval}
The gap's placement axis is earnings; we show earnings is a faithful proxy for revealed placement on two fronts.
\emph{First, an independent earnings source.} On the same institution$\times$field, PSEO (UI wages) and Scorecard
(Title-IV median) rank pay near-identically (Spearman $+0.94$, $n=1535$), and the field-level gap \emph{replicates}
across the two independent population definitions (Spearman $+0.68\,[+0.52,+0.80]$ over 57 fields;
Fig.~\ref{fig:placement}, left). Using PSEO flows, 34\% of bachelor's graduates work out-of-state, and netting destination
geography barely moves the gap ($0.68\to0.67$): the rank gap is robust to geography even though geography drives
earnings \emph{levels}. \emph{Second, non-wage placement.} Across earnings horizons (1/4/5-year) and across sources
(Scorecard, PSEO, ACS), salary standing tracks non-wage placement --- occupational prestige (a survey-based status
score) and vertical match (the inverse of underemployment) --- at Spearman $+0.65$ to $+0.84$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:placement}, right). The proxy \emph{improves} at longer horizons (salary-vs-occupational-prestige rises
$+0.75\to+0.83$ from 1 to 5 years on Scorecard, replicated on PSEO $+0.73\to+0.84$), as graduates reach the
occupations their field routes them into. Earnings is a validated revealed-placement proxy; its one systematic
incompleteness is timing (Section~\ref{sec:defer}).

\subsection{The clean channel and its mechanism: licensing sets pay by setting, not school}\label{sec:license}
One external, field-level channel cleanly moves the gap. In a precision-weighted regression of the gap on two
external anchors (ACS PUMS), occupational \textbf{licensing raises the gap, $b_{\text{licensure}}=+0.66\,
[+0.33,+0.97]$}, while academic absorption is null ($-0.02\,[-0.42,+0.46]$); the two anchors are near-orthogonal
($\mathrm{corr}=-0.21$), so the absorption null is informative, not collinearity. Additional candidate channels
(pay-wedge, public-sector share) are collinear with licensing and add nothing ($R^2$ rises only $0.27\to0.29$;
the residual ranking is unchanged at Spearman $+1.00$).

\paragraph{The mechanism.} We identify \emph{why} licensing decouples prestige from pay, and it is not wage
compression. Within-field cross-institution wage variance is \emph{not} lower in licensed fields
($\mathrm{corr}(\text{licensure},\,\text{wage CV})=+0.02$): there is real spread to predict. Instead, a commonality
decomposition of within-field earnings on prestige versus institution-state fixed effects --- using
\emph{incremental} $R^2$ to handle the geographic clustering of elite institutions --- shows that as licensure
rises, school prestige adds essentially nothing beyond geography:
\[
\mathrm{corr}\!\left(\text{licensure},\ \text{incremental prestige }R^2\right) = -0.59\ [-0.80,-0.30],
\qquad
\text{incremental geography }R^2:\ +0.47,
\]
with Nursing the clean case (incremental prestige $R^2 \approx 0$; its pay is $\sim$72\% a state phenomenon,
$\sim$0\% an alma-mater one). The reading: in regulated, locally-employed fields, pay is set by \emph{setting} ---
state, employer, shift, local pay scale --- so the school's academic prestige cannot predict pay, which
mechanically produces the prestige$\to$pay rank disagreement the gap measures (Fig.~\ref{fig:license}). Here
``licensure'' is the cleanest \emph{observable marker} of a collinear regulated/public-sector/locally-employed
bundle (the public-sector and pay-wedge channels are collinear with it); the marker, not necessarily the
credential as cause, identifies where placement is local. This is the mechanism behind $b_{\text{licensure}}=+0.66$,
field-general across the decomposable fields, not a Nursing special case.

\caption{The licensing mechanism. \emph{Left:} licensure does not compress within-field wage variance (flat).
\emph{Right:} as licensure rises, the \emph{incremental} variance explained by school prestige (beyond geography)

\subsection{Where the proxy fails: pipeline deferral is a timing fact, not undervaluation}\label{sec:defer}
The proxy's one systematic failure is timing, and it is cleanly localised. In high-deferral fields --- those whose
graduates mostly proceed to graduate school --- early-career BA earnings is a poor proxy for the \emph{terminal}
market: the field-level divergence between occupational status and BA wage is explained by the graduate-degree
share (status-residual $\sim$ deferral, Pearson $+0.59$). This is a \emph{cross-field} proxy-timing effect with
three sharp properties: it is distinct from the null academic-absorption channel (deferral is BA$\to$graduate
school, absorption is PhD$\to$academia; the two are near-orthogonal, $+0.08$); it does \emph{not} touch the
within-field gap (which is rank-based: $\mathrm{corr}(\text{gap},\text{deferral})=+0.02$ on reliable fields); and
it narrows the apparent ``decoupling'' of the natural
sciences to a measurement-timing fact rather than market undervaluation. We considered and rejected three further
alternative readings of the gap (generic-brand versus field-specific prestige; a distinct integration ``shape'';
program-level mispricing as value-added); each is dispatched in SI~\ref{si:ruleout}.

\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robust}
The four findings survive a battery of checks (full detail in SI~\ref{si:robust}). The per-field reliability
filter (signal-fraction plus bootstrap-CI screen) is the false-positive control on which all downstream analysis
runs. Three further checks confirm the structure is not an artifact of how it is built: the integrated--decoupled
ranking is not an artifact of cluster size (subsampling every cluster to a common $n$ preserves the order, Spearman
$+0.98$); it is horizon-stable (the between-discipline ICC drifts from $0.50$ at 1yr to $0.30$--$0.33$ at 4--5yr,
but the cluster ranking holds at $+0.81$--$0.85$); and prestige-rank measurement noise barely perturbs it
(propagating SpringRank sampling uncertainty widens the gap CIs by only $\sim$4\% and flips $0/16$ reliability
flags). The headline also holds under taxonomy expansion (recovering genuine fields, $54\to66$, cluster ranking
$+0.98$).

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Implication: a critique of field-agnostic rankings.} Because the prestige$\leftrightarrow$placement
structure is field-specific --- the integrated--decoupled typology --- a single overall ranking conflates fields
with very different gap structures and mis-prices programs. A field-specific ranking is both necessary and
constructible: as a demonstrator we build a CS-school ranking that blends continuous CS prestige with CS earnings;
it cross-validates (Scorecard$\leftrightarrow$PSEO CS earnings $+0.84$; Wapman$\leftrightarrow$ORCID CS prestige
$+0.69$) and flags the schools a field-agnostic ranking would get most wrong
\coauthor{feature the ranking in-text, in SI, or spin off --- see COAUTHOR\_NOTES.md}.

\paragraph{The honest framing.} Naming the second axis \emph{revealed placement} rather than ``employer
reputation'' is not cosmetic: it states the asymmetry plainly. We compare a perception (the academy's hiring-network
prestige) with a realised outcome (where graduates land), and earnings is the validated-but-partial proxy for that
outcome. This framing makes the contribution precise --- a measured, structured, mechanistically-explained wedge
between academic perception and labour-market reality --- and it scopes the open questions to the placement side.

\paragraph{The forward frontier.} Two extensions follow. \emph{Temporally}, the labour market actively reprices
fields: in real (constant-2023) dollars over 2001--2019, 46 of 60 fields rise (Statistics $+\$2{,}311$/yr,
Computer Science $+\$1{,}616$/yr; Pharmacy $-\$1{,}774$/yr), and whether academic prestige lags this revaluation is
a sharp lead--lag question we specify but do not yet identify (SI~\ref{si:dynamic}). \emph{Cross-sectionally}, the
within-field placement gap should be measured on a \emph{multi-dimensional} placement object --- employer identity
and occupation mix (resume data), occupational status, trajectory --- via the estimand
$\mathrm{gap}^{\mathrm{occ}}_f = 1-\mathrm{Spearman}_i(\text{prestige}_{i,f},\,\text{occ-prestige}_{i,f})$ that
needs institution$\times$field occupation flows. An organising two-signal employer-learning model that generates
the gap and these extensions is developed in SI~\ref{si:model}.

% =====================================================================
\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
Three limitations are load-bearing. Scorecard earnings are Title-IV only, though cross-validated against PSEO's
independent population (Section~\ref{sec:proxyval}); the analysis is \emph{descriptive}, not causal --- we
characterise the wedge but do not estimate a causal earnings effect of prestige, and median earnings cannot see
the elite tail where \citet{chetty2023diversifying} locate the brand's causal payoff; and the placement axis is,
at the institution grain, a single proxy (median earnings), so the within-field gap on a \emph{multi-dimensional}
placement object remains to be measured with resume-level occupation data (the estimand is in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}). Each is a boundary of the descriptive claim, not a threat to the four findings,
which are internal to the data we use.

\section{SI: An organising two-signal model (not validated)}\label{si:model}
We build a minimal generative model in the Altonji--Pierret spirit, as an \emph{organising lens} for the empirics,
not a validated result. Each institution's graduates carry an academic value $\theta_A$ and a market value
$\theta_M$ with field alignment $\rho_f=\mathrm{corr}(\theta_A,\theta_M)$; prestige tracks $\theta_A$ with noise;
earnings are a credential-weighted blend $w(\tau_f,t)\theta_A+(1-w)\theta_M$ whose weight decays with observability
$\tau_f$ and horizon $t$ (employer learning), compressed by licensing $\lambda_f$; the gap is
$1-\mathrm{Spearman}(\text{prestige},\text{earnings})$. With one parameter vector the model reproduces three
predictions: licensing raises the gap (full-support effect $\approx+0.64$, matching the empirical $+0.66$); academic
absorption is not in the mechanism (the cleanest hit, matching the empirical null); and prestige is the slow prior
while earnings drift (gap-change correlates $+0.71$ with divergence). Its key \emph{external} prediction --- that
the licensing-netted residual estimates valuation divergence $(1-\rho)$ --- is not confirmed: two independent
divergence proxies give opposite signs (O*NET $+0.41$ vs.\ SDR $-0.61$) and are themselves negatively correlated,
so the residual-as-divergence reading is unconfirmed. We therefore present the model as an organising framework and
keep its durable, testable content (the licensing channel of Section~\ref{sec:license} and the lead--lag direction
of SI~\ref{si:dynamic}) in the main text.

\section{SI: Three ruled-out alternative explanations}\label{si:ruleout}
Three intuitive alternative readings of the gap are tested and rejected; we summarise them here.
\emph{(a) Not generic-brand versus field-specific prestige.} On medians the academia-wide brand predicts pay at
least as well as field-specific prestige (mean $c_G=+0.45 \ge c_F=+0.40$; field reputation's advantage is negative
in 42/57 fields), consistent with \citet{chetty2023diversifying}: the brand's payoff concentrates in the elite tail
that median data cannot see. \emph{(b) Not a distinct ``shape''.} Classifying each field's prestige$\to$pay relation
as graded/threshold/flat is largely redundant with integration \emph{strength} (Spearman $+0.80$--$0.96$); median
data cannot resolve winner-take-all. \emph{(c) Program ``mispricing'' is geography and selection.} Residualizing
earnings on prestige within field surfaces over/under-valued programs, but the residual is mostly geography and
selection, not value-added: destination-state fixed effects explain $R^2=0.46$ of the residual versus $0.00$ for
academic brand, and the residual is $+0.88$-correlated with the raw within-field earnings ranking. Full numbers are
in \texttt{FIELD\_VS\_GENERIC\_RESULT.md}, \texttt{TWO\_MARKET\_INTEGRATION\_RESULT.md}, and
\texttt{MISPRICED\_PROGRAMS\_RESULT.md}.
