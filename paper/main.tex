\begin{abstract}
Academic prestige and labour-market pay are two valuation systems pricing the same human capital
(institution $\times$ field). We measure the wedge between them --- the \emph{academic--employer reputation gap},
$\mathrm{gap}_f = 1 - \mathrm{Spearman}(\text{field prestige}, \text{earnings})$ within field across institutions
--- using a continuous SpringRank reconstruction of the US faculty-hiring network \citep{wapman2022quantifying,
debacco2018physical} and College Scorecard field-of-study earnings, cross-validated against Census PSEO
\citep{scorecard, pseo}. Three descriptive results form the spine. (i) The gap is \emph{structured by discipline}:
a precision-weighted multilevel decomposition attributes $\sim$30\% of cross-field gap variance to between-CIP-2
clusters (measurement-corrected $\sim$0.45; one-way $\eta^2=0.50$), with an integrated end
(Computing, Mathematics/Statistics, Economics-adjacent social science, Engineering) and a decoupled end
(Health, Arts, natural sciences). (ii) \emph{One} clean external channel --- occupational \emph{licensing}
($b\approx+0.66$) --- plus a large irreducible residual; additional candidate channels are collinear and add
essentially nothing. (iii) The structure is \emph{robust to a completely different earnings source}: PSEO and
Scorecard rank institution pay near-identically (Spearman $+0.94$) and the field-level gap replicates
($+0.68$). We further show the labour market \emph{actively reprices fields} in real dollars over two decades
(Statistics $+\$2{,}311$/yr, Computer Science $+\$1{,}616$/yr; Pharmacy $-\$1{,}774$/yr), with a
prestige--earnings lead--lag specified but not yet identified. Three deflationary analyses are reported as
ruled-out alternatives and scope conditions: the gap is \emph{not} explained by generic-brand versus
field-specific prestige (on medians the brand predicts pay at least as well), \emph{not} a distinct
``shape'' beyond its strength, and program-level ``mispricing'' is largely geography and selection, not
value-added. We make no causal claim; we read the wedge through signalling and employer-learning theory
\citep{spence1973job, altonji2001employer, macleod2017big} and cite \citet{chetty2023diversifying} for why
median data cannot see the tail where elite-brand effects concentrate. The practical implication is a critique
of field-agnostic prestige rankings.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and stakes}\label{sec:intro}
Students, employers, and policy-makers lean on institutional prestige as a summary of educational quality.
But the dominant public signals --- overall university rankings (QS, US News overall) --- are \emph{field-agnostic}:
they compress a single ``brand'' that is supposed to stand in for the quality of every program. If the way
academic prestige maps onto labour-market pay is itself \emph{field-specific}, then a field-agnostic ranking
systematically mis-prices programs, and the literature on how rankings move behaviour
\citep{luca2013salience} implies real welfare consequences.

This paper measures the object that a field-agnostic ranking hides: the wedge, \emph{within each field across
institutions}, between where the academy places an institution (academic reputation, AR) and where the labour
market prices its graduates (employer reputation, ER). We call it the \emph{academic--employer reputation gap}.
Our contribution is deliberately \emph{descriptive}: we do not estimate the causal earnings effect of attending
a more prestigious program --- that requires administrative, tail-resolved data we do not have
\citep{chetty2023diversifying} --- but we characterise the gap, its discipline structure, the one external
channel that cleanly moves it, its robustness across two independent earnings definitions, and its movement
over time. Ruling out several intuitive alternative explanations is part of the contribution, not a failure of
it.

\paragraph{Roadmap.} Section~\ref{sec:framework} frames the gap through signalling and employer learning.
Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the data and the honest coverage limitations. Section~\ref{sec:gap} defines
the gap and reports its discipline structure. Section~\ref{sec:explain} isolates the licensing channel and
rules out alternatives. Section~\ref{sec:model} sketches a two-signal model and is explicit about its
unvalidated status. Section~\ref{sec:robust} reports the robustness battery, including the PSEO cross-source
test. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the temporal revaluation result, the lead--lag frontier, and the
ranking critique.

\section{Framework: two pricing equilibria}\label{sec:framework}
Treat an institution's graduates in a field as a bundle of human capital that two markets price independently.
The \emph{academic} market prices research/credential standing and is realised in the faculty-hiring network:
who hires whose PhDs induces a prestige hierarchy \citep{wapman2022quantifying}. The \emph{labour} market
prices productivity as employers perceive it and is realised in earnings. Signalling theory
\citep{spence1973job} says credentials carry information when productivity is hard to observe; employer-learning
theory \citep{altonji2001employer} says the weight on the credential signal decays as employers learn true
productivity. \citet{macleod2017big} show reputation can causally raise pay precisely where employer
information is scarce.

Two equilibria that price the same bundle can disagree. We define their disagreement as the gap and read a
\emph{large} gap as a field where academic standing and market pay are decoupled, a \emph{small} gap as a
field where they are integrated. This is a metaphor of two valuation systems, not a literal arbitrage claim:
there is no traded asset and no enforceable law of one price. Crucially, the gap is a \emph{rank} statistic
within field, so it is insensitive to the level differences (e.g.\ engineering pays more than education) that
dominate raw earnings; what it captures is whether, \emph{among the institutions that teach field $f$}, the
prestige ordering and the pay ordering coincide.

\section{Data and coverage}\label{sec:data}
\paragraph{Academic reputation (AR).} We use the public US faculty hiring network of
\citet{wapman2022quantifying}. The release ships only ordinal ranks; because the blend in
Section~\ref{sec:explain} and the applied ranking require meaningful score \emph{differences}, we recompute the
continuous SpringRank score \citep{debacco2018physical} on the public field-level edge subgraphs. A from-scratch
reconstruction reproduces the published ranks at a data ceiling ($\rho\approx0.77$); an independent ORCID-derived
rebuild of the hiring network reproduces Wapman's academia ranks at Spearman $0.74$ with the elite-private top
intact (Section~\ref{sec:robust}), certifying the method.

\paragraph{Employer reputation (ER).} Primary earnings are College Scorecard Field-of-Study median earnings
(bachelor's; 4-year-after-completion horizon) \citep{scorecard}. The honest limitation is that Scorecard covers
\emph{Title-IV} (federally-aided) graduates only and is a single cross-section. We cross-validate against Census
PSEO (UI-wage-based, population-inclusive of UI-employed graduates, real 2023 dollars) \citep{pseo}.

\paragraph{Join and coverage.} The Classification of Instructional Programs (CIP) is the join key. Scorecard
covers $\sim$69\% of Wapman institutions with even coverage across prestige quartiles
(74.2/64.5/71.7/65.6\% by quartile from top to bottom); PSEO covers only $\sim$29\% and is top-truncated
(just 21.5\% of the top prestige quartile; $\sim$18 of the top-20 elite privates absent), so PSEO is used for
cross-validation and flows, \emph{not} as the primary earnings source. PSEO doctoral and master's cells are
100\% disclosure-suppressed at the 4-digit-CIP grain (18{,}442 doctoral rows, 0 released), which is why the
analysis is at the bachelor's level. Table~\ref{tab:coverage} summarises.

\section{The gap and its discipline structure}\label{sec:gap}
\paragraph{Definition and reliability.} For field $f$ with institutions $i$,
\[
\mathrm{gap}_f = 1 - \mathrm{Spearman}_i\!\left(\text{prestige}_{i,f},\ \text{earnings}_{i,f}\right).
\]
Because a Spearman computed on few or noisy institutions is attenuation-prone, a diagnostic battery
(null-model, bootstrap, residualization, restriction) certifies the gap is \emph{real signal, not a global
artifact}: only 15\% of fields' observed gaps fall inside the perfect-agreement-plus-noise null band while
65\% sit above it, and the artifact channel explains only $R^2=0.20$ of cross-field gap variance
(\texttt{DIAGNOSTIC\_RESULT.md}). A per-field reliability filter (signal fraction $\ge0.5$ \emph{and} bootstrap
CI excluding the null band) is required: well-measured low-gap fields have tight clearly-positive
prestige--earnings correlations (Computer Science $0.71\,[0.62,0.78]$, Statistics $0.75\,[0.52,0.87]$), and
genuine high-gap fields survive (Biology $0.37\,[0.22,0.52]$, $N=138$), but a low-earnings-signal cluster
(Chemistry, Earth Sciences) is noise-dominated and screened out.

\paragraph{The headline: the gap is a discipline-area phenomenon.} A precision-weighted multilevel meta-model
$\mathrm{gap}_f \sim 1 + (1\,|\,\text{CIP-2})$ (REML; $V=\mathrm{diag}(\mathrm{SE}^2)+\tau^2\cdot\text{same-parent}$),
fit on $n=48$ reliable fine fields, gives between-cluster variance $\tau^2=0.0231$ (SD $0.15$ gap-units) and
\[
\mathrm{ICC} = \frac{\tau^2}{\tau^2+\overline{\text{within-cluster var}}} = 0.30,
\]
i.e.\ $\sim$30\% of cross-field gap variance is \emph{between} broad discipline areas (one-way $\eta^2=0.50$).
Correcting the within-cluster variance for sampling noise raises this to $0.45$ (Section~\ref{sec:robust}), and
expanding the field universe (Section~\ref{sec:robust}) to 0.57. The gap is primarily about \emph{which
discipline a field belongs to}, not field idiosyncrasy.

\paragraph{The integrated--decoupled typology.} The shrunk, partial-pooled CIP-2 cluster means
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cluster}) rank from integrated to decoupled:
\emph{integrated} (prestige predicts pay) --- Computer/Info $0.348$, Math \& Stats $0.352$, Social Science
$0.421$, Engineering $0.432$; \emph{decoupled} --- Health $0.753$, Arts $0.746$, Natural Resources $0.730$,
Biological Sciences $0.678$, Physical Sciences $0.661$. A weak within-cluster observability gradient (gap vs.\
earnings dispersion) is directionally negative but CI-spanning-zero (pooled Spearman $-0.44\,[-0.74,+0.01]$;
within-cluster slope $-0.527\,[-1.08,+0.02]$), a second-order modifier, not the headline.

\section{What explains the gap}\label{sec:explain}
\subsection{The one clean channel: occupational licensing}
We net mechanical channels with external, field-level anchors (ACS PUMS) in a precision-weighted regression of
the gap on licensure intensity (share of a field's graduates in licensed occupations) and academic absorption
(share of doctorate-holders in academia). \textbf{Licensing is the one clean channel:}
$b_{\text{licensure}}=+0.66\,[+0.33,+0.97]$, while academic absorption is null
($b=-0.02\,[-0.42,+0.46]$); the two anchors are near-orthogonal ($\mathrm{corr}=-0.21$), so the absorption null
is real, not collinearity. The two channels explain $R^2=0.28$ of cross-field gap variance; the rest is an
irreducible residual.

\textbf{Additional channels are redundant.} Adding a pay-wedge and a public-sector-share channel raises the
joint $R^2$ only from $0.27$ (licensure-only) to $0.29$. In the joint standardized model licensing dominates
($+0.11\,[+0.07,+0.14]$) while pay-wedge ($-0.03$) and public-sector ($-0.01$) collapse to zero --- they are
collinear compression mechanisms (public-sector's $+0.37$ bivariate vanishes once licensing is netted;
channel correlations up to $|0.54|$). The multi-channel residual cluster ranking is \emph{identical} to the
licensing-only ranking (Spearman $+1.00$) and the residual SD barely shrinks ($0.228\to0.225$). A unionization
channel is left as a documented data gap rather than fabricated. \emph{Net: the search for more external
channels finds collinear redundancy, not new explanatory power.}

\subsection{Ruled-out alternatives (scope conditions)}\label{sec:ruleout}
Three analyses test intuitive explanations and rule them out; reporting them is a strength.

\paragraph{(a) It is not generic-brand vs.\ field-specific prestige.} A natural story is that students chase
the overall ``brand'' while pay tracks field-specific academic strength (``Georgia-Tech-CS vs.\ Yale-CS'').
On \emph{median} earnings this fails: the generic academia-wide brand predicts pay at least as well as
field-specific prestige (mean $c_G=+0.45 \ge c_F=+0.40$; field reputation's advantage $\mathrm{ADV}=c_F-c_G$ is
negative in 42/57 fields; horse-race classification: brand-dominant 15 vs.\ field-dominant 7). The two prestige
signals are themselves strongly aligned ($\rho_{PP}$ mean $+0.79$), and in a data-driven CS divergence test
the brand-strong schools \emph{out-earn} the field-strong ones (\$119{,}643 vs.\ \$98{,}970). This is fully
consistent with \citet{chetty2023diversifying}: the brand's payoff is concentrated in the elite \emph{tail}
(top-1\%, elite firms) that median data cannot see. \emph{Scope condition: the gap is a median-earnings
phenomenon; brand effects live in a tail we do not observe.}

\paragraph{(b) Integration varies in strength, not in ``shape''.} Classifying each field's prestige$\to$pay
relationship as graded/threshold/flat yields mostly graded (30/5/9 of 44 fields), but the classification is
\emph{largely redundant with integration strength} (non-top-decile correlation tracks the overall correlation
at Spearman $+0.96$; the class ordinal tracks it at $+0.80$). Winner-take-all (threshold) shapes are rare on
medians --- but that is a \emph{limitation} of median data (the winner-take-all premium is in the tail), not a
finding. \emph{Scope condition: we cannot resolve shape, only strength.}

\paragraph{(c) Program ``mispricing'' is geography and selection.} Residualizing earnings on prestige within
field surfaces over/under-valued programs, but the residual is \emph{mostly geography and selection, not
value-added}: institution-level destination-state fixed effects explain $R^2=0.46$ of the residual versus
$0.00$ for academic brand, and the standardized residual is $+0.88$-correlated with the raw within-field
earnings ranking (because prestige explains only $\sim$18\% of within-field earnings). Program residuals are
horizon-unstable (29\% flip sign between 1-yr and 4-yr). \emph{Scope condition: ``where two valuations
disagree'' $\neq$ ``this program adds value''.}

\section{A two-signal model (built, not validated)}\label{sec:model}
We build a minimal generative model in the Altonji--Pierret spirit: each institution's graduates have an
academic value $\theta_A$ and a market value $\theta_M$ with field alignment $\rho_f=\mathrm{corr}(\theta_A,
\theta_M)$; prestige tracks $\theta_A$ with noise; earnings are a credential-weighted blend
$w(\tau_f,t)\theta_A+(1-w)\theta_M$ whose weight decays with observability $\tau_f$ and horizon $t$
(employer learning), compressed by licensing $\lambda_f$. The gap is $1-\mathrm{Spearman}(\text{prestige},
\text{earnings})$. With \emph{one} parameter vector the model reproduces three predictions cleanly --- P2
(licensing raises the gap; full-support effect $\approx+0.64$, on top of the empirical $+0.66$); P3 (academic
absorption is \emph{not} in the mechanism --- the cleanest hit, matching the empirical null); P5 (prestige is
the slow prior, earnings drift; gap-change correlates $+0.71$ with divergence) --- and partially reproduces P1
and P4.

\textbf{We do not present the model as validated.} Its key external prediction --- that the licensing-netted
residual estimates valuation divergence $(1-\rho)$ --- is \textsc{unconfirmed}: two independent divergence
proxies give \emph{opposite} signs (O*NET skill-content $+0.41\,[+0.08,+0.68]$ vs.\ SDR salary-wedge
$-0.61\,[-0.88,-0.11]$) and are themselves negatively correlated ($-0.64$), so the residual-as-divergence
reading is downgraded from ``estimated'' to ``unconfirmed.'' The model also fails to reproduce the
ICC-vs-horizon decline in its default calibration (an honest miss; a disclosed discipline-varying
recalibration recovers it without breaking P2/P3/P5, but that is fitting, not prediction). \emph{The model's
durable, testable content reduces to the licensing channel and the lead--lag direction
(Section~\ref{sec:discussion}); we present it as an organizing lens, not a result}

\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robust}
\paragraph{Statistical hardening.} (i) The integrated--decoupled \emph{ranking} is not an artifact of
institutions-per-cluster: subsampling every cluster to a common $n=20$ preserves the order (rank Spearman
$+0.98$ over 18 clusters), though $\mathrm{corr}(\text{gap},n)=-0.54$. (ii) Correcting the within-cluster
variance for measurement noise (observed $0.0549$, mean $\mathrm{SE}^2=0.0267$) raises the ICC from $0.30$ to
$0.45$ --- noise-correction \emph{strengthens} the between-discipline share. (iii) Propagating prestige-rank
sampling uncertainty (edge-bootstrapped SpringRank) into the gap CIs widens them by only $\sim$4\% (median
ratio $1.04$) and flips $0/16$ reliability flags.

\paragraph{Crosswalk audit.} Auditing why the 81-field Wapman taxonomy collapsed to 54: of 27 missing fields,
12 are recoverable crosswalk misses, 3 are subsumed, and 12 are genuine data gaps (graduate-only fields with no
bachelor's earnings). Recovering the 12 (54$\to$66 fields) holds the headline: cluster ranking stable
(Spearman $+0.98$), ICC raw $0.30\to0.32$, corrected $0.45\to0.57$.

\paragraph{Cross-source: PSEO.} On the same institution$\times$field, PSEO (UI wages) and Scorecard (Title-IV
median) rank pay near-identically (Spearman $+0.94$, $n=1535$), and the field-level gap \emph{replicates}
across the two independent population definitions (Spearman $+0.68\,[+0.52,+0.80]$ over 57 fields;
Fig.~\ref{fig:pseo}). Using PSEO flows, 34\% of bachelor's graduates work out-of-state at 5 years (a bound on
the UI undercount), and destination-state geography explains a modest within-field earnings share here
($R^2\approx0.13$ origin-state); crucially, \emph{netting destination geography barely moves the gap}
($0.68\to0.67$) --- the prestige$\leftrightarrow$pay rank gap is robust to geography even though geography
drives institution-level earnings \emph{levels}.

\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{pseo_vs_scorecard_gap.png}
\caption{Cross-source validation: the field-level gap from two independent earnings definitions (Scorecard
Title-IV median vs.\ PSEO UI wages), Spearman $+0.68$. Source: \texttt{PSEO\_GAP\_CROSSVALIDATION\_RESULT.md}.}

\paragraph{Field-tag robustness.} Tagging the placement population by OpenAlex \emph{research} field does not
improve per-field reproduction of Wapman (mean $\rho$ $0.515$ vs.\ degree-text $0.551$; a dead heat once
de-leaked, $0.515$ vs.\ $0.517$), because research field cross-cuts degree field (only 35--54\% of a degree
cohort lands in the matching research field) --- the residual to the data ceiling is coverage-bound, not
tagging-noise-bound.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What we have shown.} A measurable, discipline-structured wedge between academic prestige and
labour-market pay; one clean external channel (licensing) and a large irreducible residual; robustness to a
completely different earnings source; and several intuitive alternatives ruled out. The contribution is the
\emph{measurement and structure} of the wedge, not a causal mechanism.

\paragraph{The temporal contribution.} The labour market \emph{actively reprices fields} in real (constant-2023)
dollars over 2001--2019 (Fig.~\ref{fig:reval}): 46/60 fields rise, led by Statistics ($+\$2{,}311$/yr),
Computer Science ($+\$1{,}616$), Computer Engineering ($+\$1{,}421$), Biomedical Engineering ($+\$969$),
Finance ($+\$868$) and Economics ($+\$817$); the sharpest falls are Pharmacy ($-\$1{,}774$/yr) and
Communication Disorders ($-\$1{,}405$). This is a clean novel descriptive result on its own. The exciting
forward frontier is the \emph{lead--lag}: does academic prestige adjust to economic revaluation, or lag? Our
two-period prestige pilot finds within-field prestige stability of median $\rho\approx0.50$ (a noise-depressed
lower bound) against materially moving earnings --- \emph{suggestive} that prestige is the slow anchor --- but
with only two noisy periods the cross-lagged direction \emph{is not identified}. We specify, but do not run,
the finer-resolution panel a real lead--lag study needs.

\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{field_earnings_revaluation.png}
\caption{Temporal revaluation: real (2023\$) bachelor's earnings by field over graduation cohorts. The market
reprices fields. Source: \texttt{DYNAMIC\_LEADLAG\_PILOT\_RESULT.md}.}
\label{fig:reval}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Implication: a critique of field-agnostic rankings.} Because the prestige$\leftrightarrow$pay
structure is field-specific (the integrated--decoupled typology), a single overall ranking conflates fields
with very different gap structures and mis-prices programs. As a concrete demonstrator we build a CS-school
ranking that blends continuous CS prestige with CS earnings; it cross-validates (Scorecard$\leftrightarrow$PSEO
CS earnings Spearman $+0.84$; Wapman$\leftrightarrow$ORCID CS prestige $+0.69$) and flags 15 schools whose
placement is salary-driven and thus low-robustness --- exactly the entries a field-agnostic ranking would get
most wrong \coauthor{whether to feature the ranking in-text, in SI, or spin off --- see COAUTHOR\_NOTES.md}.

\paragraph{What a causal version would need.} Our earnings are medians of observed workers; we cannot see the
elite tail where \citet{chetty2023diversifying} locate the brand's causal payoff, nor do we have an exogenous
shock to reputation as in \citet{macleod2017big}. A causal version would need administrative, tail-resolved,
quasi-experimental data we do not have. We are explicit that this is the boundary of the descriptive claim.

\paragraph{Limitations.} Title-IV earnings (cross-validated, not eliminated); a selected/top-truncated PSEO
subset; selection vs.\ value-added unaddressed throughout; the model unvalidated; the lead--lag unidentified.
We report each plainly. The audit trail from every claim to its source is in \texttt{CLAIMS\_LEDGER.md}.
